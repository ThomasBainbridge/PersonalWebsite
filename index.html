01 — Projects

Personal projects

001

OpenFOAM Dam-Break CFD & Surrogate Modelling

A CFD and data-driven surrogate modelling study built on the OpenFOAM interFoam laminar dam-break case. A fully automated pipeline generates a 403-case 2D VOF simulation database — 343 structured training cases plus 60 Latin-hypercube validation cases — varying water-column height, obstacle height, and obstacle position. Robust impact metrics (rolling peak pressure, pressure impulse, and wetting time) are extracted per case, and ten scalar surrogate model families are screened to predict them, reaching R² up to 0.997 on unseen cases. A 200-mode incremental-PCA POD reduced-order model trained on ~20,900 free-surface snapshots then reconstructs the alpha.water field on unseen cases for rapid design-space exploration.

002

Reduced-Order Forecasting of Particle-Laden Flow

A scientific-machine-learning testbed that generates a controlled 2D inertial-particle dataset, converts Lagrangian particle motion into Eulerian concentration fields, and trains reduced-order PyTorch models to reconstruct and forecast the field evolution. Inertial particles are driven through an unsteady Taylor–Green vortex, reproducing textbook preferential concentration, with four diagnostics confirming St ≈ 1 as the strongest-clustering regime. POD and a convolutional autoencoder compress the fields to a 16-dimensional latent space, and a residual latent forecaster — plus a Stokes-conditioned Neural-ODE variant — rolls the dynamics forward in time, beating the persistence baseline at every Stokes number.

003

ML-Augmented RANS Modelling of Separated Flow

A CFD and machine-learning study of whether a lightweight, interpretable data-driven correction can reduce the systematic model-form error of RANS (k‑ω SST) for separated turbulent flow over periodic hills, validated against DNS. A baseline OpenFOAM failure map (reattachment x/H ≈ 7.5 vs DNS 4.5) motivates an interpretable eddy-viscosity and Reynolds-stress-anisotropy correction, learned with ridge / random-forest / gradient-boosting models and a tensor-basis neural network. The correction is coupled back into the solver through two custom-compiled OpenFOAM solvers — cutting the a-posteriori reattachment error ~78% on an unseen geometry — then subjected to honest out-of-distribution tests across geometry and completely different separated flows.

02 — University Projects

Academic work

A selection of projects and assignments completed during my undergraduate and postgraduate studies.

Laminar Channel Flow — CFD vs Analytical Study

Comparative study of analytical and numerical solutions for fully developed laminar flow in a 2D channel. An analytical velocity profile was derived from the Navier–Stokes equations and implemented in MATLAB as a benchmark. ANSYS Fluent simulations were then run across three structured mesh densities, two outlet boundary conditions, and first vs second order discretisation schemes. A non-isothermal case was also conducted to examine thermal behaviour within the flow field.

ANSYS Fluent MATLAB CFD Mesh Refinement Heat Transfer Cranfield MSc 80%

Numerical Methods for PDEs & High Performance Computing

Two-part assignment covering finite difference schemes and HPC performance analysis. A Fortran solver was developed to apply four explicit schemes (FTCS, Upwind, Lax-Friedrichs, Lax-Wendroff) to a 1D linear advection equation across two initial value problems, with error norm analysis and CFL number investigation. The second part analysed the UCNS3D CFD solver on the Taylor–Green vortex problem using 2–64 MPI processes, assessing parallel speed-up, efficiency, task breakdowns, and CO₂ footprint for both second and third order schemes.

Fortran Numerical Methods MPI HPC PDEs CFD Cranfield MSc 84%

Hybrid Grid Generation — NASA HiLiftPW-1 Trap Wing

Generation and assessment of a coarse RANS-suitable hybrid unstructured grid for the NASA HiLiftPW-1 trapezoidal wing configuration at Re = 8.5 million, following official workshop gridding guidelines. The surface mesh was built in Pointwise using an advancing front algorithm with targeted edge length clustering around high-curvature regions. Near-wall resolution was achieved using T-Rex prism layer meshing with a y⁺ ≈ 1 target, producing 38 prism layers at a growth rate of 1.2. The final grid totalled ~15.9 million cells and was assessed against skewness, aspect ratio, and orthogonal quality metrics before import into ANSYS Fluent.

Pointwise ANSYS Fluent Grid Generation RANS High-Lift Aerodynamics Boundary Layer Meshing Cranfield MSc 82%

Data Analysis & Uncertainty — Turbulent Channel Flow

Processed DNS time-series data from a turbulent channel flow to compute mean and RMS velocity profiles, benchmarked against the Lee & Moser reference dataset. Spectral analysis was performed using FFT with rectangular, Hann, and Blackman windowing strategies, alongside a moving average filter, to investigate spectral leakage and frequency content. Statistical characterisation of velocity fluctuations was carried out through skewness, kurtosis, PDF estimation, and Pearson correlation analysis, with flow visualisation completed in ParaView using Q-criterion iso-surfaces.

MATLAB ParaView DNS Signal Processing Cranfield MSc 73%

Turbulence Modelling — Virginia Tech Stability Wind Tunnel

A group RANS study simulating turbulent airflow through the high-speed leg of the Virginia Tech Stability Wind Tunnel using ANSYS Fluent, with reference conditions derived from experimental operating points across three Mach numbers. Multiple turbulence closures were compared — including k-ɛ RNG, k-ω BSL, k-kL-ω, and k-ω SST — alongside curvature correction, corner correction, and energy equation effects to assess their influence on secondary flow features. Grid convergence was quantified using Roache's GCI framework across four mesh refinement levels, and turbulence intensity and Reynolds shear stress profiles were benchmarked against DNS reference data.

ANSYS Fluent RANS k-ω SST GCI / Mesh Convergence Cranfield MSc 71%

Numerical Modelling for Compressible Flows

A Godunov-type finite-volume solver for the compressible Euler equations was extended in Fortran to implement HLL and HLLC approximate Riemann solvers alongside second-order piecewise-linear reconstruction with a minmod limiter, verified against a reference solution across three grid resolutions. Multidimensional benchmark problems — the 2D Riemann problem, Mach 3 forward-facing step, and double Mach reflection — were analysed using UCNS3D, with density, Mach number, and pressure contours used to identify wave structures and limiter activation contours used to compare CWENOZ scheme orders and reconstruction strategies.

Fortran UCNS3D Finite Volume Methods HLLC / HLL / WENO Cranfield MSc 85%

Numerical Modelling for Incompressible Flows

An in-house SIMPLE-TDMA finite volume solver was adapted from a 2D channel flow configuration to solve the benchmark lid-driven cavity problem, with physically correct boundary conditions implemented and channel-specific flow scaling removed. A grid convergence study across five mesh resolutions established an adequate cell count, with non-dimensionalised centreline velocity profiles validated against published datasets at Reynolds numbers 400, 1000, and 3200. Comparative simulations in ANSYS Fluent and OpenFOAM were used to assess the in-house solver against commercial CFD packages, with the assigned Reynolds number of 100 analysed through streamlines and velocity contours.

SIMPLE-TDMA ANSYS Fluent OpenFOAM Finite Volume Methods Cranfield MSc 79%

Individual Research Project — UAV Parameter Estimation with Universal Differential Equations

Completed with industrial partner Saab BlueBear, identifying flight-dynamics models for a 1960 mm twin-motor fixed-wing UAV from operational ArduPilot logs rather than dedicated system-identification manoeuvres. Eleven flight logs — 3.7 hours airborne — were signal-mapped and segmented into manoeuvres before longitudinal, lateral-directional and coupled 6-DOF grey-box models were fitted in Julia. A universal differential equation formulation then added a neural residual to the state equations, improving held-out validation error across the lateral-directional states by 21–42% over five random seeds, with a hardened structural ablation used to limit angle-of-attack degradation.

Julia Python MATLAB System Identification Grey-Box Modelling Universal Differential Equations Flight Dynamics Cranfield MSc
