Personal projects

001

OpenFOAM Dam-Break CFD & Surrogate Modelling

An automated CFD and surrogate-modelling study of dam-break impact loads on an obstacle. Starting from the OpenFOAM interFoam dam-break case, a pipeline generates and runs a 403-case two-phase VOF simulation database, varying the water-column height and the obstacle's height and position. Impact metrics extracted from every run train scalar surrogate models, and a POD reduced-order model reconstructs the free surface for unseen geometries.

002

Reduced-Order Forecasting of Particle-Laden Flow

A scientific-machine-learning testbed for particle-laden flow. Inertial particles are tracked through a prescribed, unsteady vortical flow and converted into Eulerian concentration fields. POD and a convolutional autoencoder compress those fields into a low-dimensional latent space, where linear (DMD) and neural (MLP, Neural-ODE, GRU) models forecast their evolution in time against a persistence baseline.

003

ML-Augmented RANS Modelling of Separated Flow

A CFD and machine-learning study of the model-form error of RANS turbulence models in separated flow. Baseline k‑ω SST simulations of flow over periodic hills in OpenFOAM are compared with DNS reference data, and interpretable machine-learning models learn a correction to the eddy viscosity and Reynolds-stress anisotropy. Two custom OpenFOAM solvers feed the correction back into the CFD, and it is tested on unseen geometries and different separated flows.

02 — University Projects

Academic work

A selection of projects and assignments completed during my undergraduate and postgraduate studies.

Laminar Channel Flow — CFD vs Analytical Study

Comparative study of analytical and numerical solutions for fully developed laminar flow in a 2D channel. An analytical velocity profile was derived from the Navier–Stokes equations and implemented in MATLAB as a benchmark. ANSYS Fluent simulations were then run across three structured mesh densities, two outlet boundary conditions, and first vs second order discretisation schemes, each compared against the analytical profile to isolate the effect of mesh, boundary treatment and scheme order. A non-isothermal case examined thermal behaviour within the flow field.

ANSYS Fluent MATLAB CFD Mesh Refinement Heat Transfer Cranfield MSc 80%

Numerical Methods for PDEs & High Performance Computing

Two-part assignment covering finite difference schemes and HPC performance analysis. A Fortran solver was developed to apply four explicit schemes (FTCS, Upwind, Lax-Friedrichs, Lax-Wendroff) to a 1D linear advection equation across two initial value problems, with error norm analysis and CFL number investigation. The second part analysed the UCNS3D CFD solver on the Taylor–Green vortex problem using 2–64 MPI processes, assessing parallel speed-up, efficiency, task breakdowns, and CO₂ footprint for both second and third order schemes.

Fortran Numerical Methods MPI UCNS3D HPC PDEs CFD Cranfield MSc 84%

Hybrid Grid Generation — NASA HiLiftPW-1 Trap Wing

Generation and assessment of a coarse RANS-suitable hybrid unstructured grid for the NASA HiLiftPW-1 trapezoidal wing configuration at Re = 8.5 million, following official workshop gridding guidelines. The surface mesh was built in Pointwise using an advancing front algorithm with targeted edge length clustering around high-curvature regions. Near-wall resolution was handled with T-Rex prism-layer meshing to a y⁺ ≈ 1 target, and the finished grid was assessed against skewness, aspect ratio, and orthogonal quality metrics before import into ANSYS Fluent.

Pointwise ANSYS Fluent Grid Generation RANS High-Lift Aerodynamics Boundary Layer Meshing Cranfield MSc 82%

Data Analysis & Uncertainty — Turbulent Channel Flow

Processed DNS time-series data from a turbulent channel flow to compute mean and RMS velocity profiles, benchmarked against the Lee & Moser reference dataset. Spectral analysis was performed using FFT with rectangular, Hann, and Blackman windowing strategies, alongside a moving average filter, to investigate spectral leakage and frequency content. Statistical characterisation of velocity fluctuations was carried out through skewness, kurtosis, PDF estimation, and Pearson correlation analysis, with flow visualisation completed in ParaView using Q-criterion iso-surfaces.

MATLAB ParaView DNS Signal Processing Cranfield MSc 73%

Turbulence Modelling — Virginia Tech Stability Wind Tunnel

A group RANS study simulating turbulent airflow through the high-speed leg of the Virginia Tech Stability Wind Tunnel using ANSYS Fluent, with reference conditions from experimental operating points at three Mach numbers. Four turbulence closures — k-ɛ RNG, k-ω BSL, k-kL-ω and k-ω SST — were compared, alongside curvature correction, corner correction and energy-equation effects on secondary flow. Grid convergence used Roache's GCI framework across four mesh levels, with turbulence intensity and Reynolds shear stress benchmarked against DNS data.

ANSYS Fluent RANS k-ω SST GCI / Mesh Convergence Cranfield MSc 71%

Numerical Modelling for Compressible Flows

A Godunov-type finite-volume solver for the compressible Euler equations was extended in Fortran to implement HLL and HLLC approximate Riemann solvers alongside second-order piecewise-linear reconstruction with a minmod limiter, verified against a reference solution across three grid resolutions. Multidimensional benchmarks — the 2D Riemann problem, Mach 3 forward-facing step and double Mach reflection — were analysed using UCNS3D, with density, Mach and pressure contours identifying wave structures and limiter-activation contours comparing CWENOZ scheme orders.

Fortran UCNS3D Finite Volume Methods HLL / HLLC CWENOZ Cranfield MSc 85%

Numerical Modelling for Incompressible Flows

An in-house SIMPLE-TDMA finite volume solver was adapted from a 2D channel flow configuration to solve the benchmark lid-driven cavity problem, with corrected boundary conditions and channel-specific flow scaling removed. A grid convergence study across five mesh resolutions set the cell count, and non-dimensionalised centreline velocity profiles were compared with published datasets at Reynolds numbers 400, 1000 and 3200. Comparative simulations in ANSYS Fluent and OpenFOAM assessed the in-house solver against commercial CFD packages.

SIMPLE-TDMA ANSYS Fluent OpenFOAM Finite Volume Methods Cranfield MSc 79%

Group Project — Design Optimisation of a Pod Deployable UAV

Multidisciplinary MSc group project designing a pod-deployable UAV for two contrasting missions — high-speed interception and long-endurance payload carriage. Four initial concepts were screened with CFD before a hybrid configuration was taken forward. The work spans a NACA aerofoil optimisation framework, an EDF versus solid-propellant propulsion study, an automated CFD-driven duct optimisation loop, full-aircraft simulations, a MATLAB/Simulink six-degree-of-freedom trim and stability campaign, and a physics-informed machine learning surrogate for aerodynamic forces and flow fields.

ANSYS Fluent Python CFD Optimisation Flight Dynamics Machine Learning Cranfield MSc 82% View Project ↗

Individual Research Project — UAV Parameter Estimation with Universal Differential Equations

Completed with industrial partner Saab BlueBear, identifying flight-dynamics models for a 1960 mm twin-motor fixed-wing UAV from operational ArduPilot logs rather than dedicated system-identification manoeuvres. Eleven flight logs — 3.7 hours airborne — were signal-mapped and segmented into manoeuvres before longitudinal, lateral-directional and coupled 6-DOF grey-box models were fitted in Julia. A universal differential equation formulation then added a neural residual to the state equations, evaluated on held-out flights across several random seeds, with structural ablations probing the neural term's contribution.

Julia System Identification Grey-Box Modelling Universal Differential Equations Flight Dynamics Cranfield MSc Currently being graded
